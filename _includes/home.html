About

I am a fourth-year PhD student at the Chester F. Carlson Center for Imaging Science at the Rochester Institute of Technology, expected to graduate in July 2027. I work with Dr. Cristian A. Linte in the Biomedical Modeling, Visualization, and Image-Guided Navigation (BiMVisGN) Lab. My research develops deep learning for medical imaging and surgical data: registration of preoperative and intraoperative images and surfaces, organ-surface completion from sparse, partial, and noisy observations, and segmentation and detection of anatomy and surgical instruments, including under non-canonical poses, partial visibility, noise, and shifts between imaging modalities.

Beyond single-task models, I am exploring surgical vision foundation models that learn shared representations across tasks and domains. Combining large-scale pretraining, multi-task learning, and vision–language modeling, the aim is one architecture that can interpret surgical scenes, recognize tools and tool–tissue interactions, identify phases and steps, and support skill and error assessment, toward context-aware assistance that improves intraoperative decisions and patient safety. Before the PhD I earned a bachelor’s degree in Computer Engineering from Sagarmatha Engineering College, affiliated with Tribhuvan University. Outside research I enjoy traveling, exploring new places, hiking, and experiencing different cultures.

Featured research

MIUA 2025

Toward patient-specific partial point cloud to surface completion for pre- to intra-operative registration in image-guided liver interventions

On two liver phantoms, completing a 30% visible surface lowered target registration error from 33.13 ± 13.89 mm and 39.87 ± 15.07 mm to 5.19 ± 1.34 mm and 3.35 ± 0.61 mm.

Paper

Healthcare Technology Letters, 2025

Assessing learning-based reconstructed liver surfaces from partial point clouds for improving pre- to intra-operative 3D to 3D registration

Error in the completed liver surface is larger farther from the visible region and can mislead both rigid and non-rigid registration, including inside the camera’s field of view.

Paper

IEEE EMBC 2026

Evaluating large vision-language models for surgical tool detection

Of Qwen2.5, LLaVA1.5, and InternVL3.5, Qwen2.5 was the strongest surgical-tool detector: better instrument recognition than Grounding DINO, which localized more accurately.

Paper

MIUA 2026

Toward mask annotation-free surgical instrument segmentation from endoscopic images using text-prompted Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3)

Prompting SAM3 with “tool,” then classifying those masks with Qwen, segments instruments without mask annotations and beats instrument-name prompts, while still trailing fully supervised methods.
